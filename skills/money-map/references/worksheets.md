# The 18 Worksheets

Read this when an owner mentions a worksheet from the book, asks for help filling one in, or when a step of the Money Map conversation needs one of these calculations.

---

## How to use this page

The book has 18 one-page worksheets across Chapters 1 to 16. Each has a "Your Move" line, the worksheet, and the Money Map box it feeds. They work with a pen alone. Free interactive versions of all 18 are at map.forgetaibook.com, where the owner can save their answers alongside their Money Map.

When you coach an owner through a worksheet:

- Ask one question at a time, in the order below. Rough answers are fine ("about 20 a year").
- Do the arithmetic for them and show the sum in one line.
- Never fill a blank with your own guess. If they don't know, mark it unknown, or state a plain assumption and label it as theirs to check.
- Every worked figure is a range or a scenario, never a promise.
- Close by saying which Money Map box it now fills.

---

## 01. What you aimed AI at (Chapter 1)

**Your Move:** Before you read on, be honest about what your AI has actually done to your money.

**Fields:** up to 5 rows, two columns: what you pointed AI at, and the number on your accounts it moved.

**Calculation:** none. Blanks in the right-hand column are the point. This whole book is about filling them.

**Feeds:** no Money Map box. It frames the book.

**Walk-through:** Ask what they've pointed AI at in the last year, then which number on their accounts each one changed. If they say "it saves time", ask where the time went. Don't judge the blanks.

## 02. Your last 3 AI spends (Chapter 2)

**Your Move:** Name the last three things you pointed AI at. For each, which line of your accounts did it move?

**Fields:** 3 rows: the spend, the task it did, the line it moved (revenue, cost to deliver, overhead or none), dollars it moved. If any answer is none, tick where the link to money broke: the target, the delivery, or the time it still needs.

**Calculation:** none.

**Feeds:** which line to aim at.

**Walk-through:** One spend at a time: what it cost, what job it did, which line moved. For each "none", ask whether it was aimed at the wrong work, never properly used, or too early to tell.

## 03. Three fates of a saved hour (Chapter 3)

**Your Move:** Pick one place AI saved time this year. Did the cost leave, did the time go to earning, or did it just evaporate?

**Fields:** where AI saved time; hours or days saved; one fate: cost removed (a role not backfilled, a contractor or overtime actually stopped), redeployed to earning (name the person and what their freed time now wins or makes), or evaporated ($0). Then dollars removed, or who was redeployed and to what.

**Calculation:** removed counts at the dollars that actually left. Redeployed is sized on the earning work it now does. Evaporated is $0. Default is evaporated. Only removed or redeployed reaches the bank.

**Feeds:** the honesty test behind every number on the map.

**Walk-through:** Ask for one example and roughly how many hours, then "What happened to that time?" If they can't name a cost that stopped or a person now doing earning work, it evaporated. Say so kindly.

## 04. Where the money is stuck (Chapter 4)

**Your Move:** Which engine is holding you back: bringing money in, making and keeping it, or what the business is worth?

**Fields:** tick the real limit: cannot get enough work in (demand, the rainmaker); cannot get enough good work out (supply, the producer and the leak); cash; or too much rides on you (the multiplier). Then why you think so, in numbers if you can.

**Calculation:** none. The one ticked is where to start.

**Feeds:** the aim, where to start.

**Walk-through:** Treat the first tick as a hypothesis. Ask for the evidence (enquiries a month, backlog, debtor days, what happens when they're away). Then test the other side: if they tick demand, ask whether they could really deliver double next month.

## 05. Worth three ways (Chapter 5)

**Your Move:** You know what your business earns. Do you know what it is worth? Work it out.

**Fields:** earnings (profit after the real running costs, before tax and one-offs); the multiple a business like yours trades at (an assumption, worth checking with an adviser); worth in three cases: market unchanged, a higher multiple you could earn, a softer market.

**Calculation:** worth today = earnings × multiple. If earnings are zero or negative, worth on earnings is nil. Never multiply a loss.

**Feeds:** Worth and the Multiple.

**Walk-through:** Ask for a normal year's earnings, checking for one-offs, grants or the owner's wage taken as drawings. Then ask what multiple applies, or offer a cautious range for their industry and size, theirs to check with an adviser. Show all three cases.

## 06. The Rainmaker (Chapter 6)

**Your Move:** Who is your rainmaker, whatever their title? Are you losing people coming in, or at the close?

**Fields:** your rainmaker(s); where the money is lost: not enough coming in, losing them at the close, or not selling again to customers you already have; your one rainmaker move (the change in the work, and what AI takes off the rainmaker); what it could add in a year, worst and best.

**Calculation:** count added sales at contribution (margin), never at revenue.

**Feeds:** Rainmaker, orders won.

**Walk-through:** Ask who actually brings the work in, then for counts: enquiries a month, quotes, signings, speed of quotes, revenue from existing customers. Find where most are lost. The move protects the human conversation and hands AI the drafting, chasing and capturing around it.

## 07. Work you can't get out the door (Chapter 7)

**Your Move:** Find the work you already win but never fully deliver, and size what realising it would add, after the cost of delivering it.

**Fields (a year):** extra work you could deliver with the people and plant you already have; your contribution margin on it (%); less any genuinely new cost to deliver it (overtime, a contractor, extra kit); less the AI, all-in (people running it, usage, help).

**Calculation:** contribution = extra work × margin %. Real extra earnings = contribution − new cost − AI all-in. Box the last line.

**Feeds:** Revenue realised and Earnings.

**Walk-through:** Ask about backlog and work turned away or late. Then the double-output test: if work doubled, who would they pull into the room, and what breaks first? Size only what current people and plant can handle, then take off each cost.

## 08. The leak, bottom-up (Chapter 8)

**Your Move:** Do not guess a total. Build your leak up, error by error, then claw back a slice.

**Fields:** a table of error types: times a year, cost to put right (materials + labour + callout + customer lost). Then the slice you could realistically claw back.

**Calculation:** gross leak = times × cost, added across rows. Recoverable = gross × slice, starting at 30% and capped at 70%. Labour hours freed go through the fates test: worth $0 until removed or redeployed.

**Feeds:** Cost to deliver.

**Walk-through:** Ask what gets redone, returned or corrected. One error type at a time: how often, and the cost to fix, including the customer who doesn't come back. Add them up and start the slice at 30% unless they have evidence for more.

## 09. Cash timing (Chapter 9)

**Your Move:** Profit is not cash. Work out whether growth will stretch you before it pays you back.

**Fields:** days of stock held; days to get paid; days you take to pay; if you grew revenue by $X, the extra working capital it ties up; cash you could free by collecting faster or holding less; your lowest cash point in the next 12 months.

**Calculation:** net still to fund = cash tied up − cash freed.

**Feeds:** the Cash strip, which sits underneath the map and is never added to earnings or worth.

**Walk-through:** Ask the three day counts, then the growth they're planning and what it ties up, then what faster collection could free. End on the lowest cash point.

## 10. Multiple scorecard (Chapter 10)

**Your Move:** If the person who holds the most in their head walked out for a month, what would break?

**Fields:** score each weak, building or strong: easy to govern (documented, transferable, not dependent on you); easy to grow (road ahead, customers not over-concentrated); easy to predict (repeat or recurring revenue, next year not a guess). Then your one move to lift it.

**Calculation:** none. The weakest score is where the multiple leaks.

**Feeds:** the Multiple.

**Walk-through:** Start with the Your Move question. Then one per score: what's written down, the biggest customer's share of revenue, how much of next year is booked or recurring. Agree the weakest, then the move.

## 11a. Knowledge in heads (Chapter 11)

**Your Move:** What does your business know that lives only in people's heads, and would hurt most to lose?

**Fields:** a table: the knowledge, who holds it, what breaks if they leave, whether it's written down anywhere (Y/N).

**Calculation:** none. Start with the one that would hurt most.

**Feeds:** stands alone (institutional memory), and informs "easy to govern".

**Walk-through:** Ask who they'd least want to lose for a month, then what that person knows that nobody else does. One row at a time. Rank by hurt.

## 11b. What should tap you on the shoulder (Chapter 11)

**Your Move:** Name the one thing you check obsessively because you do not trust it to tell you when it goes wrong.

**Fields:** a table of things checked all day: what going wrong would look like, who should be told the moment it does. Then the first thing to wire into a control room so they can stop checking it.

**Calculation:** none.

**Feeds:** stands alone (the control room).

**Walk-through:** Ask what they check first thing each morning. For each, ask what "wrong" looks like and who needs to know. Pick the one that eats the most of their attention.

## 12. The running calculation (Chapter 12)

**Your Move:** Put your own numbers over the manufacturer's, and run the two levers as a scenario, not a promise.

**Fields, Now:** orders won, revenue realised, cost to deliver, overhead, multiple. **Your one move**, carried from an engine worksheet. **Next:** earnings after the move; the multiple (same, or higher if the business is easier to govern, grow and predict); worth three ways: market unchanged, evidenced improvement, softer market. **Cash:** growth ties up, faster collection frees, one-off setup.

**Calculation:** margin = revenue − cost to deliver. Earnings = margin − overhead. Worth = earnings × multiple (nil if earnings are zero or negative). Net cash still to fund = tied up − freed. Cash is never added to worth.

**Feeds:** the whole Money Map.

**Walk-through:** Fill Now line by line and reconcile the totals. Take the move and its gain from the engine worksheet, and run Next as a scenario on their assumptions. Close with: the gap between worth today and worth after is a sensitivity check on your own assumptions, not a prize you are owed.

## 13a. Your money path (Chapter 13)

**Your Move:** Do not start from a pain list. Walk how the money actually moves, and find the leak nobody questions.

**Fields:** the stages attract, enquire, quote, win, start, deliver, invoice, collect, keep. On each, mark a delay, a handoff, a re-key, rework or a blind spot (blank if it flows). Then the empty chair: the leak everyone treats as just the weather.

**Calculation:** none.

**Feeds:** the one move.

**Walk-through:** Ask them to walk one real, recent job from first hello to last payment, stage by stage: how long, and what it waited on. Then ask what everyone just accepts: no-shows, unbilled work, jobs that quietly don't convert.

## 13b. The start test (Chapter 13)

**Your Move:** Before you spend, run your move through three questions.

**Fields:** the move being tested, then Y/N on: 1. Is the likely result clearly worth more than the cost? 2. Is the worst case one you could live with, including the cash it ties up? 3. Does the first, smallest step test the thing you are least sure about?

**Calculation:** all three yes, start. Any no, it is the wrong move, or there's more homework on the prize.

**Feeds:** the one move.

**Walk-through:** One question at a time. For each no, ask what would turn it into a yes, usually a smaller first step or a cheaper pilot.

## 14. Ratio and adoption (Chapter 14)

**Your Move:** Two numbers: your turnover-to-wages ratio, and how honest you have been with the people who have to use this.

**Fields:** operating profit; total wages (the printed worksheet asks for turnover and wages); who has to use this; Y/N: had the honest conversation, anyone reads it as a threat to their job, the last rollout actually got used; realism factor 0.3 to 1.0; your forecast for the move (a year).

**Calculation:** ratio = profit ÷ total wages, using a normal year's operating profit (lift it by growing the top, not shrinking the bottom). The printed worksheet uses turnover ÷ wages, which the book says only stands in for the sharper profit gauge while margins hold. If the owner has already filled in the turnover version, keep it and work out the profit version beside it. Counted = forecast × realism factor, with the factor held between 0.3 and 1.0.

**Feeds:** discounts the move to what is real.

**Walk-through:** Do the ratio, then the adoption checks one at a time. Set the factor from their answers: 0.3 if past rollouts never changed anything, close to 1 only if proven. If the boxes are unticked, the most valuable move is the conversation, not the build.

## 15. Size the spend (Chapter 15)

**Your Move:** Take the one initiative you are closest to spending on, and size it honestly before you spend.

**Fields:** the initiative; the range it could move in a year, worst and best; full running cost a year (the build and the person to run it, not the licence); cash it needs and cash it frees; expected payback in months; review point (how long to give it before judging it).

**Calculation:** none built in. Then run the start test from 13b.

**Feeds:** the one move and its worth.

**Walk-through:** Ask what they're closest to buying. Size worst and best at margin, not revenue. Push for the full running cost, including the person who runs it. Agree a review date before they start.

## 16. Your first move (Chapter 16)

**Your Move:** This week, name the one constraint you already suspect, check it with your people, and run the start test.

**Fields:** the one constraint you suspect is holding your money back; who does that work, and what they said when you checked; Y/N: worth it, downside affordable, first step tests the unknown; your one action this week.

**Calculation:** none.

**Feeds:** points back to the Money Map.

**Walk-through:** Ask for the constraint in one sentence. Ask whether they've checked it with the person who does that work, and if not, make that the action. Run the three questions. End with one action they can do this week, with a day attached.
