# The money path and the one move

Read this when you know roughly where the constraint is and need to walk one real job through the business, find where the money stalls, choose the single move to make first, and test it before the owner commits a dollar.

---

## Core ideas

**Walk the money path first, in the right order.** The book's practical method in Chapter 13 is simple: get the people who do the work to walk you through it, step by step, the real thing, not the abstract version. A phone recording is enough. But follow the order, or you drown in detail:

1. Start where a customer first comes into contact with the business, and walk their journey through to the sale and, where it matters, on to how you keep them.
2. Then follow how the work actually gets done and delivered, because that is where the promise turns into money.
3. Only then fill in the rest of the business around it.

Map it **exactly as it happens now, warts and all**. Not the process manual. Not how it should work.

**The stages.** Worksheet 13a lays the path out left to right:

attract → enquire → quote → win → start → deliver → invoice → collect → keep

**What to mark at each stage.** Five things:

- a **delay** (work waiting on someone or something)
- a **handoff** (work passing between people, where it can stall or drop)
- a **re-key** (the same information typed in again)
- **rework** (doing it twice because it was wrong the first time)
- a **blind spot** (nobody can see what is happening, or nobody owns it)

**The empty chair.** This is the book's name for the leak everyone treats as just the weather. In the dentistry story it was literal: a patient cancels or does not show, and a dentist sits idle in an empty chair, earning nothing the business can ever get back. Across most centres that was simply accepted. In the owner's business it might be quotes that go quietly cold, prospects who said "yes, but later" and were never followed up, work done but never billed, or slots that sit empty.

Be clear with the owner: the empty chair is **a hidden leak in the process, not a person**. It is never a person who is supposedly not pulling their weight. It is money already in reach that the business has stopped noticing. In the dentistry story the fix came from a person (a receptionist's habit), and the leak was a process everyone else had accepted.

**Choosing the one move.** What you are hunting for is "the one move with the highest impact on the money that you can actually pull off". Not the most exciting one. The one where a real change in the profit meets a change the business can genuinely make. Describe it as a change in the work: who owns it, what AI does in it, and what the freed person does instead.

**What breaks first.** Also ask the harder question: if the business keeps growing the way it has, what breaks first, and what would that cost? A bottleneck that is only annoying today can cap the business a year from now. Sometimes that future break is the right one move.

**Warm-up versus the prize.** If AI is genuinely new to the business and the team has barely touched it, the book says not to start with the big prize. Start by getting everyone up to speed: put a good assistant in their hands, set a few simple ground rules for using it safely, personalise it to the business, and let people get a quick, satisfying win. That builds the appetite and trust the real work needs. But be clear it is the warm-up, not the prize. If the team already uses AI tools and pays for subscriptions, the work starts at the map.

## Questions to ask the owner

**Walking the path (pick one real, recent job)**
- Think of a job you won in the last month or two. How did that customer first find you?
- What happened next, and who did it? How long did it take? What did it wait on?
- At which steps does information get typed in again, or passed to someone else?
- How many enquiries drop out at each step? Where do quotes go quiet?
- Once the work was won, how long until it started? Was anything redone?
- How long from finishing the work to sending the invoice? From invoice to cash in the bank?
- Did that customer buy again? Did anyone ask?

**Finding the empty chair**
- What does everyone in your industry just accept as how it works?
- Where is money you have already earned, or nearly earned, quietly slipping away?
- Is there one person, team or site that gets noticeably better results doing the same work? What do they do differently?

**Choosing the move**
- Of the stuck points we found, which one moves the most money?
- Which of those could you realistically change in the next few months, with the people you have?
- What would the freed person do with their time instead?
- How new is AI to your team? Have people actually used it?

## How to size it

Put a rough range on each stuck point before choosing: a worst case and a best case a year, plus any cash it ties up or frees (cash is separate, never added to earnings). Then test the chosen move.

**The one-page start test (Chapter 13, long form).** Three plain questions that fit on one page:

1. **What are we changing, and why?** The outcome that matters, what is stopping it now, who owns the process and the result, and what the freed time or capacity will actually go and do.
2. **What will it cost, and what will it leave us?** The extra money it makes after the cost of making it, the real savings, what it costs to set up and to run, and when cash will be needed during the change.
3. **How will we know whether to keep going?** The one assumption most likely to be wrong, a small test to check it, the result measured in dollars not hours, and the date it will be reviewed.

**The yes/no version (Worksheet 13b, repeated in Chapters 15 and 16).**

1. Is the likely result clearly worth more than the cost? Y / N
2. Is the worst case one you could live with, including the cash it ties up? Y / N
3. Does the first, smallest step test the thing you are least sure about? Y / N

All three yes: start. Any no: it is the wrong move, or there is more homework to do on the prize. Use the long form to think the move through and the yes/no version as the final gate.

## Examples from the book

**The dentistry group (Chapter 13, real, anonymised).** The empty chair came from walking the business and reading the systems, not from the team's pain list. One centre billed about 30% more per dentist per day than its near-identical twins because a receptionist followed patients up and refilled cancelled slots 2 days ahead. The move was to build her habit into a system for the whole group. Turn-up rates and billable hours per dentist rose. No dollar result is given in the book.

**The proposal tool (Chapter 16, the author's firm, real).** The author's firm runs every org-wide AI investment through a 10 minute Money Map exercise: which line does this move, by how much, and what does it cost? A clever skills mapper took a back seat because it moved no line that mattered. A duller proposal-writing tool moved to the front: turnaround went from 3 days to 30 minutes, the job came off the developers, and the solutions team could walk clients through rough numbers live. The firm started closing around 40% more work, roughly 5 times faster. That is the one move chosen on money, not on how impressive it looks.

## Traps to avoid

- **Mapping the ideal, not the real.** If the owner describes how it should work, ask what happened on the last actual job.
- **Mapping everything at once.** Follow the money path first; fill in the rest later.
- **Blaming a person for the empty chair.** The leak is a process the business has stopped seeing. Pointing at a person sends the fear underground (see `your-people.md`).
- **Picking the exciting move.** The one move is highest impact that the business can actually pull off, not the coolest demo.
- **Skipping the warm-up when it is needed, or mistaking it for the prize.** A team that has never used AI needs a quick win first; a team that already has the subscriptions should go straight to the map.
- **A first step that tests nothing.** The smallest first spend should test the assumption the owner is least sure about.
- **Measuring in hours.** The start test asks for a result in dollars, with a review date.

## Where it lands on the Money Map

- The stuck points fill **Bottlenecks** (engine, what happens, and a rough range a year).
- The chosen change fills **The one move** and **Where AI works**.
- The start test answers fill **Start test**, and its third question shapes **First step** and **Review**.
- **Worksheets:** Worksheet 13a (walk your money path, mark delays, handoffs, re-keys, rework and blind spots, name the empty chair) and Worksheet 13b (the three-question start test). The start test is repeated on Worksheets 15 and 16.
